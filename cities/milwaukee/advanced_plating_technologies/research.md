**Trend:** on-site releases peaked at 20,584 lb in 2022 and fell to 10,213 lb in 2024, because 1-bromopropane dropped from 19,064 lb (2022) to 8,705 lb (2024).

**Background:** founded in 1948 as Artistic Plating Company and now run by the fourth generation of the family. It runs nine plating lines in three buildings on one city block near Fiserv Forum, plating gold, silver, nickel, copper, tin and more for medical, defense and telecommunications customers.

**About 1-bromopropane:** EPA added it to the Clean Air Act list of hazardous air pollutants (final rule published January 5, 2022, the first addition since 1990). Under TSCA, EPA found it presents an unreasonable risk to health (neurotoxicity, developmental toxicity and cancer) and proposed restrictions in August 2024. Whether those restrictions are final has not been confirmed.

**Where it goes / what it touches**
- **Air:** 1-bromopropane, nitric acid, cyanide, nickel and lead reach downtown and the Haymarket area.
- **Sewer → Lake Michigan:** about 29,100 lb of nitrates plus small amounts of metals go to MMSD for treatment. MMSD discharges from Jones Island (to the Milwaukee Outer Harbor) and South Shore (to Lake Michigan); which plant receives this flow still needs to be confirmed.
- **Water:** about 0.2 mile from the Milwaukee River (USGS hydrography). No direct releases to water.
- **People within 1 mile:** 32,562 residents. 49% people of color, 44% low-income, 5% under age 5, 9% seniors.
- **Schools within 1 mile (20, the most on this map):** including Golda Meir School, Roosevelt Middle, Lincoln Middle, Cass Street Elementary, Siefert Elementary, Elm Creative Arts Elementary, Carver Academy and Milwaukee College Preparatory School (Lloyd Street). The NCES count also includes youth education programs at the Milwaukee County Youth Education Center and the Milwaukee Secure Detention Facility. 97 within 3 miles.
